# Topology Sees Sequence:
# Kernel Dimension of the Kinematic Sheaf is a Step Function of Proline Content

**Draft opening — 4 August 2026**

## Abstract

We model a cyclic peptide backbone as a discrete kinematic connection with values in SE(3). The differential of the monodromy map $d\mu$ has kernel equal to the space of infinitesimal closed motions. When every residue carries a two-dimensional domain this kernel has the expected dimension $2N-6$. Once proline is treated as a genuine dimensional reduction—φ constrained, only ψ free—the domain itself becomes sequence-dependent. For cyclic pentapeptides the kernel dimension falls in exact steps with proline count:

$$
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
\#\mathrm{Pro} & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\
\hline
\dim\ker d\mu & 4 & 3 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 0
\end{array}
$$

Topology therefore sees sequence. Scalar stiffness changes leave the kernel invariant and only rescale the positive spectrum of the kinematic Laplacian; dimensional reduction of the local configuration space moves the topological count itself. The construction recovers earlier abelian sheaf models as the equal-rank, abelianised special case and isolates the precise overlay that restores sequence dependence to the topology while remaining faithful to rigid-body closure.

## Significance in one paragraph

Previous cellular-sheaf treatments of ring closure, whether abelian or kinematic, concluded that sequence composition is invisible to the topological invariants and appears only in the metric (the positive spectrum). That conclusion holds for pure stiffness rescaling. It fails as soon as residues are allowed to carry different intrinsic dimensions. Proline is not merely a stiffer spring; it is a reduction in the dimension of the local configuration space. When that reduction is admitted at the level of the domain of $d\mu$, the dimension of the space of infinitesimal closed motions becomes a direct, step-like function of proline content. The topological obstruction to ring closure is therefore sequence-dependent. This supplies a sharp, computable distinction between topological and metric contributions of sequence to macrocycle strain and gives a geometric foundation for why proline-rich cycles are qualitatively harder to close.

## Status

Numerical confirmation for $N=5$ across multiple sequence families is complete. Hard domain reduction is the only overlay found that moves kernel dimension; metric and soft-constraint overlays affect only the spectrum. The result is ready for insertion into a full manuscript that replaces the sequence-blind claim of the preceding abelian draft.
